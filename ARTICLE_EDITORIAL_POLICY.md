# Ketogenic Research — AI Editorial Policy

This policy is binding for automatically generated research articles.

## Source hierarchy

1. PubMed metadata and abstract are the minimum source.
2. When a PubMed Central full text is available, it may be used for a fuller article.
3. No unsupported external facts may be introduced.
4. If the source is abstract-only, the output must be a short Research Note and must explicitly say that the interpretation is based on the abstract.

## Writing rules

- Use neutral, scientific, non-promotional language.
- Never invent numbers, endpoints, sample sizes, effect sizes, methods, limitations, conflicts, or conclusions.
- Distinguish study findings from editorial interpretation.
- Do not turn association into causation.
- Do not make clinical recommendations unless the source itself supports them, and even then report them as the authors' conclusion rather than as site advice.
- State the study design and population when available.
- State important limitations found in the source.
- Explicitly identify uncertainty and what remains unknown.
- Avoid sensational or clickbait titles.
- Do not imply endorsement by Ketogenic Research.
- Preserve disease, intervention, comparator and outcome terminology accurately.
- Include PMID and DOI when available.
- Produce English and Italian versions from the same evidence base.
- The Italian version must be a scientific translation, not a looser rewrite.

## Article depth

### Full text available
Create a Research Analysis with:
- Key finding
- Background
- Study design
- Main results
- Interpretation
- Strengths and limitations
- Clinical relevance
- What remains uncertain
- Source

### Abstract only
Create a shorter Research Note with:
- Key finding
- Study design
- Main result
- Cautious interpretation
- Limitations of this note
- Source

The text must explicitly state:
"Interpretation is based on the PubMed abstract; the full text was not available to this automated workflow."

## Automatic verification

A second AI pass must compare the draft against the supplied source and return PASS only when:
- all quantitative claims are supported;
- study design and population are described accurately;
- no unsupported clinical recommendation appears;
- limitations are not fabricated;
- source identifiers are correct.

Anything that fails verification must not be published or promoted.
